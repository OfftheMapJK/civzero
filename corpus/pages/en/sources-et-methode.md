CivicNode gathers practical knowledge to get through an outage and gain autonomy with what is already there. This page explains where the texts come from, how we check them and why they take this form. It applies to every page of the site.

## 1. What you are reading

- **Everything is a draft.** The skills are written with the help of artificial intelligence, then checked against their sources. None has yet been reviewed by a professional or repeated by testers: do not apply them as safe.
- **A skill is neither medical nor legal advice.** For health, care and personal safety, emergency services and professionals remain the reference.
- **Each status has a precise meaning**, which depends on where the skill comes from (see part 3).

## 2. Where the texts come from

The text of a skill only draws on sources we are allowed to adapt:

- the **public domain**, including publications of US federal agencies such as the CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) and FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency), which are not protected by copyright in the United States;
- the **CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA** licences, such as Wikipedia;
- for data, the French administration's **Licence Ouverte** and OpenStreetMap's **ODbL**.

The skills are published under **CC BY-SA 4.0**: anyone can copy, translate, print and adapt them, provided they credit CivicNode and keep their own changes open. That is the condition for drawing on Wikipedia, and for knowledge to remain a commons.

**What we do not adapt:** “non-commercial” (NC), “no derivatives” (ND), “all rights reserved” content, or content whose licence is not stated. Much of it is excellent, such as World Health Organization guides or official sites with no licence shown. We cite them and point to them under “Further reading”, without copying or closely following their text. A fact belongs to no one: we can use it if an open source confirms it, in our own words.

## 3. How we cross-check

- **One step, one source.** Each step of a skill names the source it comes from (s1, s2…), listed at the bottom of the skill with its title, author, link, licence and the date we read it.
- **No figure without a source.** A duration, a dose or a quantity that does not appear in an adapted source is marked “TO CHECK”. A unit conversion (gallons to litres, Fahrenheit to Celsius) is marked too, until reviewed.
- **Two sources where possible.** We compare at least two independent sources for safety steps, and say so when only one exists.
- **Differences are written down, not hidden.** When two sources disagree, the skill says so, explains where the difference comes from and states which choice it makes. For example, for a water supply: the CDC counts about 3.8 litres per person per day for drinking, cooking and a little hygiene, the French guide 6 litres for three days, for drinking only. The skill keeps the more cautious quantity and cites the other.
- **Recent and official sources first.** Between two instructions, we follow the more recent one from public health and safety bodies, and flag the older one.
- **A recorded review.** Each review is written down: what was checked, what was corrected, what is left for a person to do.

**We do not create knowledge, we give it a usable form.** Checking an adapted skill therefore means first checking that it is true to its sources: not word for word, since we rephrase, but without changing the meaning, without adding a figure or a task, without removing a precaution. Four reviews follow one another:

1. **The writing** applies the method and lists each figure with its source.
2. **The checker**, a program, checks the form, the licences, the source of each step, and looks for each figure in a copy of the sources.
3. **A review by another AI**, which did not write the skill, compares it step by step with those copies and flags any difference, any addition, any missing precaution. The skill becomes *checked by AI*.
4. **A person** checks the flagged points and a few others at random, then confirms. The skill becomes *true to its sources*.

An **orange** skill also needs a review by a competent person in the field (*reviewed*). A **red** skill stays under moratorium until two professionals have reviewed it. A skill **created by CivicNode**, with no authoritative source, cannot be “true to its sources”: it must be *tested* by someone other than its author, then *reproduced* by three people. Any skill, finally, gains trust when testers repeat it.

## 4. Why the skills take this form

- **One skill, one method.** Boiling, chlorinating and disinfecting with sunlight are three skills, because they need neither the same equipment nor the same precautions.
- **Checkable steps.** Each step has an action and a check you can observe: you know whether you can move on to the next one.
- **“If you get stuck”.** Foreseeable problems and their solution are written next to the step where they occur.
- **Three markers.** *Time* (an instant, hours, seasons), *tier* (the minimum equipment) and *danger* (green, orange, red) let you choose before you start. The danger is that of the main task; a riskier variant is flagged next to it. The levels are defined once, in Nodule Zero.
- **A moratorium.** Some skills are not published until professionals have reviewed them: nothing on live 230-volt circuits, no opened appliances, no combustion that gives off gases indoors, no medical procedures.
- **Our relationship with living things.** Each skill says whether it takes from, preserves or regenerates, what happens to the waste, and where it leads.

## 5. The other pages

- **Topics.** A topic's *principle* states what all its skills have in common; it is written and reviewed by hand, and contains no figure without a source. The *choice table* is calculated from the skills and the catalogue. Planned skills appear in it, greyed out, to show the whole range, even what is not written yet.
- **Leads (Spore).** These are ongoing research and trials, not methods: they cite their sources and the people working on them, and state what remains uncertain.
- **Images and diagrams.** We first reuse existing media under an open licence, checked on the file's own page. We only draw a diagram when none exists; it carries the note “draft diagram, not yet reviewed” until it is reviewed. A photo is never enough to identify an edible plant.
- **The station assistant.** It only answers from the skills. What it writes is flagged as such; levels, rules and steps are quoted exactly as written.

## 6. Help us correct

A mistake, a better source, a trial that worked or failed: each skill has a “Send a report” button, and you can also write to hello@civicnode.eu. A documented failure improves a skill as much as a success. The name of someone who tested a skill is only published with their consent.

The detailed method, for contributors, is in the project repository: `agent/METHODE.md`.
