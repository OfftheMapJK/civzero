# Find somewhere to charge your phone during an outage

An outage is rarely total: a few streets away, or in a building with a backup generator, the power is still on. It is often the fastest solution when you have neither a power bank nor a car.

## 1. Principle

Find a working socket rather than produce energy. Take your cable and your charger, charge first the phone that receives alerts, and make the most of the trip to also charge a power bank if you have one.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: GREEN (circle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Variant with a neighbour who has a generator: orange.
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Neighbour variant: a generator never runs indoors, nor in a garage, nor near an open window. Its fumes contain carbon monoxide, odourless and deadly.
> - Do not run an extension lead from a neighbour's generator through a window or in the rain.
> - During a storm or a flood, do not go out to charge a phone: follow the authorities' instructions.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Prepare what you take

- **Action:** take the cable, the mains charger and, if possible, a multi-socket adapter to share a socket with other people.
- **Check:** the cable and the charger are in the bag.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 2: Listen for where the shelters are

- **Action:** during a long outage, the town or the regional authorities may open shelters or charging points. Listen for them on the radio (*Listen to the radio to stay informed* skill) or ask at the town hall. [TO CHECK: existing schemes and how they are announced]
- **Check:** you know an open place and its opening hours.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 3: Look outside the area without power

- **Action:** look a few streets away to see whether street lights, shops or windows are lit: the outage may only affect one neighbourhood. Stations, shops, libraries or buildings with a backup generator may have accessible sockets.
- **Check:** you have found a working socket.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 4: Ask a neighbour who is equipped (orange variant)

- **Action:** a neighbour with a generator or a solar installation may agree to charge a few phones. Check that the generator runs outdoors, away from openings.
- **Check:** the generator is outside; the phone is charging.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED: official instructions on generators]

### Step 5: Charge usefully

- **Action:** stay long enough to get the charge well up rather than coming back often. Charge first the phone that receives alerts, then a power bank if you have one. Then apply the *Keep a phone charged* skill to make the charge last.
- **Check:** the priority phone is well charged before you leave.
- **Source:** not applicable

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 2 — no shelter is announced.** Ask at the town hall, or the fire brigade or police if they are present, without calling emergency numbers for this question.
- **Step 3 — the whole area is without power.** Save the charge (*Keep a phone charged* skill, steps 1 and 2) and keep the radio as your source of information.
- **Step 5 — too many people for too few sockets.** A multi-socket adapter lets you share a socket; everyone charges their priority phone, not all their devices.
