# Charge your phone from a car

A parked car holds a 12 V battery and a socket to use it. It is often the nearest energy reserve when the power goes out, as long as two rules are followed.

## 1. Principle

The car's 12 V socket, with a USB adapter, charges a phone like a power bank. Two risks: carbon monoxide if the engine runs in an enclosed space, and a flat starter battery if you charge for a long time with the engine off.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: ORANGE (triangle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Never run an engine in a garage or enclosed space, even with the door open. Exhaust fumes contain carbon monoxide, which is odourless and deadly.
> - Charging for a long time with the engine off can drain the starter battery: the car will not start again.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Get the car into the open air

- **Action:** the car must be outdoors, away from the windows and air intakes of the house, before the engine is ever started.
- **Check:** the car is outside, with no garage or closed shelter around it.

### Step 2: Plug in the adapter

- **Action:** plug a USB adapter into the 12 V socket, then the phone. Depending on the vehicle, the socket may only be live with the ignition on.
- **Check:** the adapter's light comes on and the phone shows that it is charging.
- **Source:** vehicle manual

### Step 3: Limit the time with the engine off

- **Action:** with the engine off, charge only one phone at a time and for a limited time. [TO CHECK: reasonable charging time with the engine off before risking the starter battery]
- **Check:** the car starts normally after charging.
- **Source:** vehicle manual

### Step 4: Charge with the engine running, outdoors only

- **Action:** if the starter battery is low, run the engine while charging, car outdoors, without sitting in the closed cabin.
- **Check:** the car is outside and someone is keeping watch.
- **Source:** vehicle manual

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 2 — the car's socket gives nothing.** Turn the ignition on without starting, or check the socket's fuse in the vehicle manual.
- **Step 3 — the car won't start.** The starter battery is flat: ask for a jump start from another car with cables, or wait for help.
