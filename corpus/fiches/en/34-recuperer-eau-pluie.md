# Harvest rainwater

Every year a roof receives a lot of water that goes down the drain. A tank set under a downpipe keeps some of it for the garden and for cleaning, without touching the drinking water network.

## 1. Principle

Rainwater collected on a roof is not safe to drink: it picks up dust in the air, then whatever lies on the roof and in the gutters (bird droppings, leaves, metals, fibres). In France, it is classed as “water unfit for human consumption”. This skill describes the simplest set-up, the rain tank with no connection: a tank completely separate from the house plumbing, for outdoor uses. You screen the inlet, divert the first water of each rain, close the tank, mark it “Not drinking water” and keep it up.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: GREEN (circle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Do not drink this water, do not cook with it, do not wash dishes or yourself with it: these uses are forbidden in France. Rainwater can look clean and still carry germs and chemicals.
> - Never connect the tank to the drinking water network. Contaminating the public network is punishable by 3 years in prison and a €45,000 fine.
> - Mosquitoes: an open tank becomes a mosquito breeding site, including for the tiger mosquito. Keep the tank covered and the inlets screened.
> - Falls: do not climb on the roof. The work is done from the ground, on the downpipe.
> - Weight: a full tank is very heavy. Set it on flat, stable ground, out of the way, and stop children from climbing on it or lifting its lid.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Ask the town hall

- **Action:** ask the town hall whether the municipality or the department sets rules for tanks (covering, protection against mosquitoes) and whether it helps with buying a rain tank or hands them out.
- **Check:** the town hall's answer is written down, with the date.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 2: Look at the roof and the gutters

- **Action:** from the ground, find out what the roof, the gutters and the downpipes are made of. A roof or gutters containing asbestos, lead or copper can release them into the water. Choose a part of the roof where nobody walks.
- **Check:** the material of the roof and that of the gutters are known and written down.
- **Source:** s2 (asbestos, lead, copper) and s1 (roof not accessible to people; asbestos fibres) [TO CHECK: the skill does not say which roofs are excluded by the arrêté of 12 July 2024, which could not be opened; the repealed 2008 arrêté (s3) excluded asbestos cement and lead for indoor uses]

### Step 3: Choose where the tank goes

- **Action:** place an above-ground tank near a downpipe, close to the roof, to make collection easier.
- **Check:** the tank sits under a downpipe, and its tap stays within reach.
- **Source:** s1 (above-ground tank, close to a roof or gutters)

### Step 4: Fit a screen where the water comes in

- **Action:** fit a screen or filter where the water enters the system, to stop leaves, debris and insects, and to keep mosquitoes from laying eggs.
- **Check:** no opening of the tank is left without a screen or a lid.
- **Source:** s2

### Step 5: Divert the first water of each rain

- **Action:** fit a first flush diverter: a simple device that sends the first water of each shower, the most loaded with dirt, germs and chemicals, somewhere other than the tank, especially after several dry days. The volume to divert depends on the roof area.
- **Check:** at the start of a shower, water does not flow into the tank straight away.
- **Source:** s2 [TO CHECK: volume to divert according to roof area, not given by the source]

### Step 6: Connect the tank, with no link to drinking water

- **Action:** connect the downpipe to the tank and close the lid. Do not run any pipe between the tank and the house plumbing or the public network. Do not pour rainwater into a drinking water supply: it would contaminate it.
- **Check:** following each pipe that leaves the tank with your eyes, none reaches a drinking water tap or pipe.
- **Source:** s1 and s2

### Step 7: Mark it “Not drinking water”

- **Action:** fix a “Not drinking water” sign with a clear pictogram to the tank's tap. For a rain tank with a connection, this is compulsory, and the tap must also be clearly identified and lockable, with an opening tool that is not left on it. For a rain tank with no connection, like the one in this skill, the source does not require it.
- **Check:** someone who does not know the set-up understands, without being told, that this water is not for drinking.
- **Source:** s1 (rules for the rain tank with a connection)

### Step 8: Use the water for what is allowed

- **Action:** keep this water for watering the vegetable garden and green spaces of the property, cleaning outdoor surfaces, washing the car at home and decorative fountains. The CDC advises, where possible, using mains or bottled water rather than rainwater to water the plants you eat and to rinse fruit and vegetables.
- **Check:** the tank water is not used for anything where it could be swallowed.
- **Source:** s1 (uses allowed without connection) and s2 (plants you eat, fruit and vegetables). The two sources disagree on the vegetable garden: s1 allows it, s2 advises avoiding it where possible.

### Step 9: Keep it up

- **Action:** once a year, inspect the whole set-up. Regularly: replace the filters, work the tap, drain and clean the tank, repair what is broken. If an opening of the tank has no screen, empty it at least every 10 days. Never add antifreeze.
- **Check:** the date of the last inspection is written on the tank or in a notebook, and there are no larvae or thick deposit in the water.
- **Source:** s1 (yearly visual inspection, regular upkeep, no antifreeze) and s2 (screen, or emptying at least every 10 days)

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 2 — the roof or the gutters contain asbestos or lead, or the material is unknown.** Do not install a tank on this part of the roof before asking the town hall or the regional health agency for advice.
- **Step 5 — no first flush diverter.** Leave the downpipe disconnected from the tank at the start of the first rain after a dry spell, then reconnect it.
- **Step 9 — mosquito larvae in the tank.** Empty the tank onto the ground, away from drain grates, clean it, then screen every opening before using it again.
- **The tank water smells bad or turns green.** Drain it, clean the tank and the filters, and move the tank into the shade if it lets light through.
- **You want to supply the toilets or the washing machine.** That is a rain tank with a connection: it must be declared to the town hall and follows stricter rules. This skill does not describe it.

## 5. Resources

### What the sources say

Service-Public.fr distinguishes the rain tank with no connection, kept for outdoor uses, from the rain tank with a connection, which can supply the toilets, floor washing and the washing machine and must be declared to the town hall. In both cases, drinking the water, cooking, washing dishes and washing yourself with it are forbidden (source s1). The CDC explains where the germs and chemicals in rainwater come from, and describes the screen, the first flush diverter and upkeep (source s2). It considers drinking rainwater after testing and treatment; this is not allowed in France, and this skill does not describe it.
