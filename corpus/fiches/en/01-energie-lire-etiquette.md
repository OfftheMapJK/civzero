# Read a label — battery, charger, device

Know what a device uses and what a charger supplies, without plugging anything in. It is the basis of almost every energy skill.

## 1. Principle

Every power supply has a label. On it you read:

- **Input**: what the device receives. **Output**: what the charger or battery supplies.
- **Voltage** in volts (V), **current** in amps (A), **power** in watts (W). Power = voltage × current.
- **The type of current**: alternating, symbol `~` (mains); direct, a solid line above a dashed line (batteries and most chargers).
- **The capacity** of a battery, in amp-hours (Ah, mAh) or watt-hours (Wh).
- **The polarity** of a round connector: a small diagram shows whether the centre is + or −.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Find the label

- **Action:** look under the device, on the back, on the charger block or on the battery. It may be moulded into the plastic.
- **Check:** you can read at least the word “Input” or “Output”, or a value in V.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 2: Tell input from output

- **Action:** on a charger, find the Input line (what it takes from the socket) and the Output line (what it gives the device).
- **Check:** you know which value concerns your device: it is the charger's output.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 3: Recognise the type of current

- **Action:** look for the `~` symbol (alternating) or the direct-current symbol next to the voltage.
- **Check:** you can say “alternating” or “direct” for each line.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 4: Work out the power

- **Action:** multiply the voltage by the current. Example: 12 V × 1.5 A = 18 W.
- **Check:** you have written down the device's power in watts.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 5: Read the polarity

- **Action:** find the three-part diagram (circle, centre dot, + and − signs) near the output voltage.
- **Check:** you know whether the centre of the connector is positive or negative.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 6: Read a battery

- **Action:** note the nominal voltage and the capacity. Convert to watt-hours: capacity (Ah) × voltage (V). Example: 12 V and 7 Ah give about 84 Wh.
- **Check:** you know the stored energy in Wh.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 7: Check that a charger suits a device

- **Action:** compare the charger's output with the device's input. The voltage must be the same, as must the polarity and the type of current. The current the charger can supply must be at least what the device asks for: a charger able to give more amps forces nothing, the device only takes what it needs. [TO CHECK: exceptions, especially USB chargers that negotiate their voltage with the device]
- **Check:** all four conditions are met, or you do not plug in.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 1 — the label is worn off.** Look for the model reference (often on another side or under the battery) and the manual. Never guess a voltage.
- **Step 6 — a power bank only shows mAh.** These mAh are counted at the internal cell voltage, often 3.7 V, not at the 5 V of the USB socket. Example: 10,000 mAh × 3.7 V ≈ 37 Wh. [TO CHECK: reference voltage depending on manufacturers]
- **Step 7 — the voltage matches but the connector does not fit.** Do not force it and do not adapt the connector within this skill.
