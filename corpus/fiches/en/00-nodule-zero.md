# Nodule Zero — principles and safety

The prerequisite for every skill. It defines the danger levels the interface shows, and the habits to have before any action. Electrical thresholds are left blank until a professional has written them.

## 1. Principle

CivicNode gathers practical knowledge to get through an outage and to become more autonomous with what already exists. Every skill follows the same format and carries four pieces of information to read before you start:

- **The danger level** (green, orange, red), always shown with a word and a shape: circle, triangle, octagon.
- **The status**: draft → documented (sources checked by a person) → tested → reproduced by three people.
- **The time**: instant (a few minutes, with what is at hand), hours (from an hour to a weekend of work: gathering, buying, trying, assembling), seasons (weeks, months, or the time of living things).
- **What the skill needs in advance**: a purchase, a download, an assembly.

Two kinds of source exist side by side and are never confused: **CivicNode skills**, with their status, and **external references** (Wikipedia and other embedded collections), which CivicNode makes available without having checked them.

**Red moratorium.** No skill in the beta describes:

- work on 230 V or on any live electrical installation;
- opening a plugged-in device or a power supply;
- pyrolysis, gas generators or any combustion that produces gases;
- a medical procedure;
- any access to other people's power grid, data networks or radio frequencies.

A skill with any step at red level is not published.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Know where to cut the power before you start

- **Action:** find how to cut the source you will work on: unplug, switch off, remove the fuse, disconnect the battery.
- **Check:** you can point to where to cut it, without searching.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 2: Work in the dry

- **Action:** dry hands, dry floor, no liquid within reach on the workbench.
- **Check:** nothing damp between you, the tool and the circuit.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 3: Take off any metal you wear

- **Action:** remove rings, bracelets, watch and chain before working near a battery.
- **Check:** bare hands and wrists.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 4: Never be alone when handling a battery

- **Action:** tell someone, or work in pairs, for any orange skill.
- **Check:** another person knows what you are doing and can step in.

## 4. If you get stuck

- **I don't know which level applies.** Treat the situation as the highest possible level, and do nothing until the doubt is cleared.
- **A skill contradicts Nodule Zero.** Nodule Zero comes first. Report the skill.
- **Someone has had an electric shock.** Do not touch the person until the source is cut. Call 112 (European emergency number) or your local emergency number. [TO CHECK: detailed procedure, to be taken from a first-aid course]

## 5. Resources

### Poisons and end of life

- **Never burn** cables, plastic or circuit boards: the fumes are toxic.
- **Lead-acid battery:** contains lead and acid. Do not open it, keep it upright.
- **Lithium battery:** fire risk if pierced, crushed, swollen or short-circuited.
- **Capacitors:** can stay charged after unplugging. Do not open devices (red moratorium).
- Every end-of-life device follows the skill *What to do with a dead battery or device*.
