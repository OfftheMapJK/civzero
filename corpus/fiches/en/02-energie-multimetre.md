# Use a multimeter — voltage and continuity

Two measurements are enough for most skills: checking that a wire or a fuse conducts, and reading the voltage of a battery. Current (in amperes) is not covered here.

## 1. Principle

- **Continuity:** the multimeter sends a small current and beeps if the circuit is closed. Use it **only on a disconnected circuit**.
- **Voltage:** the multimeter measures the difference between two points, without letting any significant current through. Use it **in parallel** with the source.
- Most mistakes come from the wrong position of the selector or the leads.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: ORANGE (triangle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - A multimeter set to amperes (A), or with the red lead in the A socket, placed on a battery creates a short circuit: spark, blown multimeter fuse, leads heating up.
> - Set to continuity or resistance (Ω) and placed on a battery, it does not create a short circuit, but it gives a meaningless reading and can be damaged.
> - Red moratorium: no measurement on a mains socket or a 230 V installation in the beta.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Identify the sockets

- **Action:** find the COM socket (black lead), the V/Ω socket (red lead) and the A or mA socket. In this skill, the red lead **never** goes into the A socket.
- **Check:** black in COM, red in V/Ω.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 2: Check the device

- **Action:** check that the battery works (the screen lights up) and that the leads are neither cut nor stripped.
- **Check:** readable screen, lead insulation intact along its whole length.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 3: Test the continuity of a cable or a fuse

- **Action:** circuit disconnected. Selector on the sound symbol. Touch the two probes together (it should beep), then touch the two ends of the cable or fuse.
- **Check:** beep = current flows. Silence = break.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 4: Measure the voltage of a 12 V battery

- **Action:** selector on DC voltage (V with a solid and a dotted line). On a device with manual ranges, if the voltage is uncertain, start with the highest range, then go down to the range just above the value read (often 20 V for a 12 V battery). Red probe on the + terminal, black probe on the − terminal.
- **Check:** a positive value is displayed. A − sign means the probes are reversed, which is harmless.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 5: Put it away

- **Action:** remove the probes, set the selector back to OFF, leave the red lead in V/Ω.
- **Check:** the next person will not find the device set to amperes.
- **Source:** not applicable

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 3 or 4 — the screen shows “1” or “OL”.** Value out of range or open circuit. Change the range, or check the contact of the probes.
- **Step 4 — the value keeps jumping.** Weak multimeter battery or poor contact.
- **Step 4 — the device beeps when touching the battery.** The selector was left on continuity. Remove the probes and switch to DC voltage.
- **Step 4 — spark when touching the battery.** The red lead was in the A socket or the selector on amperes. Remove immediately, check steps 1 and 4. The multimeter's internal fuse may have blown. [TO CHECK: replacing the internal fuse]
