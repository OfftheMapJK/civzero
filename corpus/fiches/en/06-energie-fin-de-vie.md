# What to do with a dead battery or device

The skill that carries the built-in ecology: what is no longer used must leave the loop without poison and without fire risk.

## 1. Principle

Batteries and electronic devices contain toxic materials and, for lithium, a fire risk. They go neither in the bin nor in a fire: they follow collection channels. Before that, ask whether the object can be repaired or give spare parts.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: ORANGE (triangle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Swollen, punctured or hot lithium battery: fire risk.
> - Batteries stored loose: their terminals can touch and create a short circuit.
> - Lead-acid battery: corrosive acid, keep it upright.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Identify the type

- **Action:** read the label (*Read a label* skill): alkaline battery, lead-acid battery, lithium battery (Li-ion, LiPo, LiFePO4), electronic device.
- **Check:** each object carries a type marking, or is classed as “unknown”.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 2: Throw nothing away, burn nothing

- **Action:** take these objects out of household waste and never burn them.
- **Check:** they are stored separately.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 3: Insulate the terminals

- **Action:** cover the terminals of batteries and flat cells with adhesive tape before storing them together.
- **Check:** no metal terminal is exposed.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 4: Set a damaged lithium battery apart

- **Action:** do not charge it, and never puncture or crush it: a puncture causes an internal short circuit that can set it on fire. Store it away from anything that burns. [TO CHECK: container and storage place; suggestion received: a non-airtight metal box, on a bed of dry sand]
- **Check:** it is alone, set apart, in an unoccupied place.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 5: Take it to a collection point

- **Action:** drop off cells and batteries in the bins of shops that sell them, and electronic devices at a recycling centre or in a shop. [TO CHECK: take-back obligations in force in France]
- **Check:** the object has been dropped in the right bin.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 6: Before throwing away, repair or salvage

- **Action:** offer the device to a repair café or a workshop, or keep its useful parts (cables, chargers, cases).
- **Check:** you have decided for each object: repair, salvage, or drop off.
- **Source:** not applicable

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 1 — no type is shown.** Treat the object as a lithium battery: terminals insulated, stored apart.
- **Step 4 — the battery smokes or heats up.** Move away and follow the Nodule Zero instructions.
- **Step 5 — no collection point available during an outage.** Store according to steps 3 and 4 and drop off later.
