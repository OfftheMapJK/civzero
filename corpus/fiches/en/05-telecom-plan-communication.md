# Communication plan for family and neighbours

The first collective skill: decide in advance how you will reach each other and where you will meet when phones stop working. No tools are needed.

## 1. Principle

When networks go down, what is missing is not the tool but the prior agreement: who to contact, by what means, at what time, where to meet. A written, shared plan replaces dozens of calls that won't get through.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: GREEN (circle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill: personal data. On the shared paper, write only first names, numbers and meeting points, with everyone's consent, and no home addresses. Do not share the plan outside the group.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Form the group

- **Action:** list the household, close family and a few immediate neighbours, with their consent.
- **Check:** every person listed has said yes.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 2: Choose a distant relay person

- **Action:** name someone who lives far away and gathers the news: a local outage will probably spare them.
- **Check:** the whole group knows their name and number.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 3: Set two meeting points

- **Action:** a nearby place (outside the building, the square) and a fallback place (a community hall, a relative's home).
- **Check:** everyone knows how to get there on foot.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 4: Set contact times

- **Action:** agree on fixed times when everyone turns on their phone or goes to the meeting point, for example morning and evening.
- **Check:** the times are written on the plan.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 5: Put the means in order

- **Action:** in this order: text messages rather than calls (they get through better and free up the lines), written messages left at the meeting point, then radio if the group has it (wave 3).
- **Check:** the order is written on the plan.
- **Source:** “Tous responsables” guide (texts rather than calls)

### Step 6: Write the plan on paper

- **Action:** one page per person: members' first names, numbers, relay, meeting points, times, order of means. No home addresses.
- **Check:** every person has their paper copy.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 7: Test it once

- **Action:** run a drill: one evening, without phones, everyone goes to the meeting point at the agreed time.
- **Check:** the whole group met up, and the plan was corrected if needed.
- **Source:** not applicable

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 1 — some neighbours don't want to join.** The plan also works for a single household. Offer it again later.
- **Step 4 — the times don't suit everyone.** Keep one shared time and leave written messages for the others.
- **Step 7 — nobody came.** The plan isn't well enough known: read it through together before running the drill again.
