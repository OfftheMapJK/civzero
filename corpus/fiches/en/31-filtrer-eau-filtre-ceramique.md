# Filter water with a ceramic filter

A filter holds back soil, particles and most germs, but it does not make water safe on its own. It prepares clear water, which you then disinfect.

## 1. Principle

The very fine pores of ceramic stop dirt, bacteria and parasites. Viruses, which are smaller, pass through. A ceramic filter does not remove chemicals either; some models have an activated carbon core that reduces part of them. So the rule is always the same: clear, filter, then disinfect (*Boil* or *Disinfect with chlorine* skills).

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: ORANGE (triangle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Believing filtered water is safe to drink: viruses pass through the filter, you must disinfect afterwards.
> - An invisible crack, after a fall, lets dirty water through: a filter that has been dropped must be checked, or replaced.
> - If the clean side touches dirty water, hands or a cloth, the filter no longer protects until it has been disinfected.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Clear the water first

- **Action:** filter cloudy water through a clean cloth, a paper towel or a coffee filter, or let it settle and draw off the clear water at the top.
- **Check:** the water poured into the filter is fairly clear: it will clog the ceramic less quickly.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 2: Choose the filter

- **Action:** read the instructions: to hold back bacteria, the pores must be 0.3 micron or smaller; for parasites, 1 micron or smaller. A pot or candle ceramic filter, or a portable filter, is suitable if it reaches these sizes.
- **Check:** the instructions give a pore size of 0.3 micron or less.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 3: Set up and fill

- **Action:** place the filter on the lower container, clean and if possible fitted with a tap, and pour the cleared water into the top part.
- **Check:** the water drips into the lower container without spilling over the sides.
- **Source:** s2

### Step 4: Disinfect the filtered water

- **Action:** disinfect the filtered water to kill the remaining viruses and bacteria: boil it or chlorinate it.
- **Check:** the filtered water has been boiled or chlorinated before drinking.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 5: Maintain the filter

- **Action:** when the water flows more slowly, gently brush the ceramic with a soft brush and rinse with clean water, without touching the clean side with dirty hands or a cloth. Replace carbon cartridges according to the instructions.
- **Check:** the flow is back, and the clean side has only touched clean water.
- **Source:** s2

### Step 6: Check for cracks

- **Action:** after a fall or a knock, examine the ceramic against the light. A cracked candle can be replaced without changing the whole filter.
- **Check:** no crack is visible; otherwise, the candle is replaced.
- **Source:** s2

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 3 — the water passes very slowly.** The ceramic is clogged: clear the water better in step 1 and brush the filter (step 5).
- **Step 5 — the clean side has touched dirty water.** Do not drink this water. Disinfect the lower container and the inside of the filter before reusing it. [TO CHECK: disinfection method depending on the model]
- **Step 2 — the instructions do not give the pore size.** Treat the filter as a simple clarifier and always disinfect the water afterwards.

## 5. Resources

### What the sources say

The CDC points out that portable filters do not hold back all germs, nor viruses, and that disinfection must be added after filtering (source s1). Wikipedia describes the two types of ceramic filter, pot and candle, and the risk of cracks and contamination of the clean side (source s2).
