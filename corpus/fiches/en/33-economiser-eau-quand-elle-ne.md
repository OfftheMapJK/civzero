# Save water when the taps run dry

When the tap gives nothing, the water left at home has to last until the network comes back or until the next supply. You save on uses, not on what you drink.

## 1. Principle

Start by knowing how much safe water is left, then keep it for what goes through the mouth and the hands: drinking, cooking, brushing teeth, washing hands. You do not ration drinking water: you reduce your needs instead, by moving less and staying cool. Water that is not safe to drink is used for the uses that tolerate it, never for drinking.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: GREEN (circle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Dehydration: going without drinking to make the supply last is more dangerous than finishing it. Children, breastfeeding women, sick people and everyone in hot weather need more water.
> - Water containing fuel or toxic chemicals is no use, neither for drinking nor for washing: no treatment makes it safe.
> - Water from the heating circuit (radiators) and water from the toilet bowl are never drunk.
> - Do not drain the hot water tank: the task involves electricity or gas, and this skill does not describe it (red moratorium).
> - Hands poorly washed for lack of water spread disease: handwashing is one of the uses to keep.
> - Doubtful water is not used for washing dishes, brushing teeth, washing or preparing food, making ice or baby formula.
> - In case of diarrhoea, stay hydrated with safe water or an oral rehydration solution, contact the nearest health facility, and keep breastfeeding a baby.
> - Wash yourself, children, nappies and clothes away from drinking water sources, at least 30 metres away [TO CHECK: conversion of 100 feet]; avoid floodwater and wash your hands after touching it.
> - After a flood, well water may be contaminated.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Take stock of the safe water left

- **Action:** first count the bottled water and the supply. Then find the other sources at home: ice cubes made with safe water, and the liquid in canned fruit and vegetables. The sources also list the water in the hot water tank; this skill leaves it out, because draining it involves electricity or gas (red moratorium).
- **Check:** a written list gives an approximate quantity for each source.
- **Source:** s2, cross-checked with s4

### Step 2: Keep the toilet cistern as a last resort

- **Action:** note the water in the toilet cistern (never the bowl) as a last resource, only if it is clear and no cleaning block or product that colours the water has been put in it.
- **Check:** the water in the cistern is clear and colourless, and the cistern contains no product.
- **Source:** s2 [TO CHECK: the sources differ, the 2004 FEMA 477 guide (s5, reference only) rules out the toilet cistern; to be settled with a reviewer]

### Step 3: Listen to instructions and protect the water in the pipes

- **Action:** listen to the local authorities' instructions (radio, town hall). If they report contamination of the network, close the home's main water valve so that polluted water does not enter the pipes.
- **Check:** everyone in the household knows where the main valve is; it is closed if the instruction was given.
- **Source:** s2

### Step 4: Keep safe water for the essentials

- **Action:** drink the water you know is safe first. Use bottled, boiled or disinfected water for drinking, cooking, making ice, brushing teeth, washing dishes, washing hands and preparing baby formula. If you cannot treat doubtful water, wait as long as possible before drinking it, without becoming dehydrated.
- **Check:** the safe water containers are stored separately, and the whole household knows what they are for.
- **Source:** s1, s2, s3 and s4

### Step 5: Drink as much as you need, move less

- **Action:** do not ration drinking water, unless the authorities tell you to. To use less, reduce physical activity and stay cool. A normally active person needs about 2.8 litres of fluid a day [TO CHECK: conversion of three quarters of a gallon], water and other drinks included; in hot weather, this need can double.
- **Check:** everyone drinks several times a day, and no one skips a drink “to save water”.
- **Source:** s1 (three quarters of a gallon a day)

### Step 6: Leave out alcohol and caffeinated drinks

- **Action:** do not count alcohol or caffeinated drinks (coffee, tea, caffeinated sodas) as water: they dehydrate.
- **Check:** each person's main drink is water.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 7: Keep handwashing

- **Action:** wash your hands with soap and safe water before eating or cooking, before feeding a child, before and after giving care, after using the toilet or changing a nappy. Without water and soap, use a hand sanitiser containing at least 60% alcohol.
- **Check:** soap and safe water, or a bottle of hand sanitiser, are placed near the toilet and the cooking area.
- **Source:** s3

### Step 8: Give non-drinking water its place

- **Action:** use water from a swimming pool or a spa for washing the body and cleaning, never for drinking. Only use water found outside (river, pond) after treating it. For collected rainwater, the sources differ: FEMA says not to drink it, the CDC lists it among waters that can be made safe by treating them. This skill follows the more cautious one: do not drink it.
- **Check:** no container of non-drinking water is stored with the drinking water.
- **Source:** s2 and s4

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 1 — there is hardly any safe water left.** Treat water you find (*Make water safe by boiling it* or *Disinfect water with chlorine* skills) and ask the town hall where water is being distributed.
- **Step 5 — someone refuses to drink to save water.** Recall the rule: you save on uses, not on drinking. Children, elderly or sick people drink first.
- **Step 7 — no soap and no hand sanitiser.** Keep a little safe water to rinse your hands at the moments listed in step 7, and look for soap as a priority.
- **Tap water comes back cloudy or coloured.** Do not drink it until you have heard the authorities' instructions; meanwhile, treat it.

## 5. Resources

### What the sources say

Ready.gov says not to ration drinking water without instructions from the authorities, and to reduce your needs by limiting activity and staying cool (source s1). The CDC keeps bottled, boiled or disinfected water for drinking, cooking and hygiene, and lists the sources of water at home (source s2); FEMA gives a similar list (source s4). The CDC lists the moments when handwashing must not be skipped (source s3).

No adapted source describes reusing the same water for several uses, from the cleanest to the dirtiest, or how to do without the toilet flush: these tasks will make a separate skill, *Reuse water and do without the toilet flush* (coming soon), written from an open source.
