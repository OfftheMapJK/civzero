# Find water nearby and know if it is safe

When the tap gives nothing and the supply runs low, there is still water outside: rain, a spring, a well, a stream. None of it is drunk as it is. This skill helps you choose the least bad one and set aside those that no treatment can save.

## 1. Principle

Water whose quality you do not know is water that is not safe to drink. Your eyes are not enough: clear water can carry germs or chemicals with nothing to show it. So you judge water by where it comes from and what surrounds it, before looking at it and smelling it. Germs are killed by boiling or disinfecting the water; fuels, metals, salts and most chemicals are removed neither by boiling nor by chlorine. Public network water is monitored; private water, such as a well's, is not checked regularly. Network water is what you look for first.

## 2. Safety

> **LEVEL: ORANGE (triangle)** — [Official label shown from Nodule Zero]
>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Do not drink water whose quality you do not know. Any water found outside is treated before drinking, and also before using it to wash or brush your teeth.
> - Water containing fuel or toxic chemicals is no use, neither for drinking nor for washing: no treatment makes it safe.
> - After a flood, well water may be contaminated.
> - Drowning and falls: do not lean over a well, do not go down a steep or slippery bank, do not go alone, keep children away.
> - A well, a spring or a pond on private land belongs to someone: ask before taking water.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Look first for monitored water

- **Action:** listen to the local authorities' instructions about water. In ordinary times, the results of the health checks on the municipality's tap water are posted at the town hall and on its website, and published municipality by municipality on the Ministry of Health's website; a summary note is sent with the water bill every year.
- **Check:** you know the current instructions, and you know where to read the results of the checks on your municipality's water.
- **Source:** s1 (instructions from the authorities) and s3 (town hall, bill, ministry website)

### Step 2: Mark the water points on the map

- **Action:** mark on a map of the area the water you can reach on foot: whatever collects rain, streams, rivers and other running water, ponds and lakes, natural springs, wells. For each one, write who it belongs to if you know.
- **Check:** the map shows at least two water points, with the walking time to reach them and the date; it is kept with the water supply and shared with the neighbours.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 3: Look at what lies upstream and around

- **Action:** for each water point, look for what could pollute it, upstream for running water, all around for a well or a spring: livestock and spreading of manure or slurry, a septic tank or a sewer, a landfill, a fuel tank or store, fertilisers and pesticides, runoff from a town or an industrial building. Write on the map what you see next to the water point. A well can also contain naturally occurring arsenic, radon, uranium or fluoride, which looking around does not show: one more reason to ask for the test (step 6).
- **Check:** each water point on the map is marked “nothing seen around” or with the list of what was seen.
- **Source:** s5 (sources of pollution, arsenic and radon), cross-checked with s6 (livestock, uranium, fluoride) and s4 (what a well must be kept away from) [TO CHECK: s4 gives minimum distances for digging a well in France, 35 metres from livestock, a sanitation system or a fuel or chemical store, 200 metres from a landfill; these are siting rules, not a safety threshold for drinking, and the skill does not present them as such]

### Step 4: Prefer running water

- **Action:** between two surface waters, prefer running water to still water.
- **Check:** the water you take comes from a place where you can see the current.
- **Source:** s2

### Step 5: Set aside water that cannot be saved

- **Action:** do not take water with floating material, a dark colour or a doubtful smell. Set aside any water that may have received fuel or toxic chemicals: it cannot be made safe. Do not drink water from a well that has been flooded or may be contaminated: it must first be disinfected and its water tested, and you should ask the health department for advice [TO CHECK: in France, the regional health agency]; meanwhile, drink bottled water or water from another safe source.
- **Check:** the water kept has no dark colour or doubtful smell, and nothing floats on its surface.
- **Source:** s2 (floating material, dark colour, smell; flooded well to disinfect and test), s1 (fuel, chemicals) and s5 (contaminated well: health department, bottled water meanwhile)

### Step 6: For a well, ask whether it has been tested

- **Action:** ask the owner of a well whether the water has been tested by an approved laboratory, and when. In France, when you create a well whose water is used for drinking, cooking, washing dishes, washing yourself or watering the vegetable garden, this test is compulsory and its result goes with the declaration. Private water is not checked regularly: with no known test, treat this water like any water you find.
- **Check:** you have seen the test result and its date, or you have written “not tested” on the map.
- **Source:** s4, cross-checked with s6 (private water is not checked regularly)

### Step 7: Collect and clear the water

- **Action:** collect the water in a clean container. If it is cloudy, let it settle, then filter it through a clean cloth, a paper towel or a coffee filter. Keep the settled and filtered water in clean, covered containers.
- **Check:** the water in the container is clear, and the container is covered.
- **Source:** s2

### Step 8: Treat before drinking

- **Action:** boil or disinfect the water before drinking it, cooking, making a drink, washing dishes or brushing your teeth, following the *Make water safe by boiling it* or *Disinfect water with chlorine* skills. Remember that these treatments remove neither heavy metals, nor salts, nor most chemicals: they do not replace steps 3 and 5.
- **Check:** no water you found is drunk without going through one of these two skills.
- **Source:** s1 and s2

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 1 — no instructions reach you.** Listen to the radio (*Listen to the radio to stay informed* skill) and ask the neighbours or the town hall; meanwhile, go on to step 2.
- **Step 2 — no water point within walking distance.** Collect rain in clean containers set out in the open, and treat it like any water you find.
- **Step 3 — every water point has a source of pollution upstream.** First set aside those exposed to fuel or chemicals, which no treatment removes. For the others, collect rain instead, and ask the town hall or the regional health agency which water to use.
- **Step 7 — the water stays cloudy after settling and filtering.** Let it settle longer and filter it again, or change water point.
- **Someone is ill after drinking water you found.** Stop drinking that water and call a doctor or the emergency services.

## 5. Resources

### What the sources say

The CDC lists the water you can find outside, all to be treated, and sets aside water containing fuel or chemicals, which no treatment makes safe (source s1). The EPA asks you to prefer running water to still water, to avoid water that is dark, smells or carries floating material, to disinfect and test a flooded well, and recalls that boiling and disinfection destroy neither heavy metals, nor salts, nor most chemicals (source s2). The CDC lists what pollutes a well, including natural substances such as arsenic or radon (source s5) and recalls that private water is not monitored regularly (source s6). Service-Public.fr says where to read the results of health checks on tap water (source s3) and recalls that a well created for drinking water must be tested by an approved laboratory (source s4).

Public fountains, standpipes and developed springs, announced in the catalogue, are described by no adapted source: they belong to the local layer (town hall, regional health agency).
