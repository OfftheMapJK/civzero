# Disinfect water with sunlight (SODIS)

A transparent bottle, clear water and a sunny day are enough to kill most germs. The method costs nothing and needs no fuel, but it takes patience and very clear water.

## 1. Principle

The SODIS method (*solar water disinfection*) combines two effects of the sun: ultraviolet rays pass through clear plastic and damage germs, and heat strengthens their effect. It targets germs: bacteria, viruses, parasites. It removes neither chemicals nor heavy metals. It only works on clear water, in a thin layer, and for long enough.

## 2. Safety

>
> Risks specific to this skill:
> - Cloudy water, or too short an exposure, stays contaminated while you believe it is safe.
> - SODIS does not remove chemicals: water polluted by industry or farming is still to be avoided.
> - Germs can multiply again if treated water waits a long time: drink it within the following day. [TO CHECK: storage time]

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Prepare the bottles

- **Action:** take transparent, colourless PET plastic bottles, or glass ones, of 2 litres or less, with few scratches. Remove the labels and wash the bottles before first use. Do not use coloured bottles or polycarbonate ones (recycling code 7).
- **Check:** the bottles are colourless, clean and unlabelled.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 2: Check that the water is clear enough

- **Action:** fill a bottle, stand it on a newspaper headline and look through it from the neck. If you cannot read the letters, filter the water or let it settle first (*Filter water with a ceramic filter* skill, step 1).
- **Check:** the headline can be read through the bottle.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 3: Fill and shake

- **Action:** fill the bottle three quarters full, close it, shake it for 20 seconds, then fill it completely and close it again.
- **Check:** the bottle is full and tightly closed.
- **Source:** s1

### Step 4: Expose to the sun

- **Action:** lay the bottles in full sun, with no shade, preferably on a metal sheet or a surface that reflects light. Leave them for 6 hours in sunny weather, or 2 full days in cloudy weather.
- **Check:** the bottles stayed in the sun for the whole time, and you noted the start time.
- **Source:** s1, s2

### Step 5: Drink from the bottle

- **Action:** drink the water straight from the bottle or pour it into a clean glass, when you drink it.
- **Check:** the treated water is not poured into a doubtful container.
- **Source:** [TO CHECK: storage instructions after treatment]

### Step 6: Replace the bottles

- **Action:** replace heavily scratched bottles or bottles that have turned cloudy: they let less light through.
- **Check:** the bottles in use are still clearly transparent.
- **Source:** s1

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 4 — it rains all the time.** SODIS does not work in continuous rain: boil the water, or collect rainwater (*Harvest rainwater* skill, coming soon).
- **Step 2 — the water stays cloudy after filtering.** Let it settle longer, or use another method: cloudy water is poorly disinfected by the sun.
- **Step 1 — no transparent bottles.** Clear PET water or soda bottles are suitable; coloured bottles are not.

## 5. Resources

### What the source says

In sunny weather (less than half the sky covered), 6 hours are enough; in cloudy weather (from half to fully covered, with little or no rain), it takes 2 days. Above a certain turbidity (30 NTU), the water must be filtered or settled first: this is what the newspaper test checks (source s1). The CDC gives the same durations (source s2).
