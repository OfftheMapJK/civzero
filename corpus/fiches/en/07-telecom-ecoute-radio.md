# Listen to the radio to stay informed

Receive news without a mobile network or Internet. Listening needs no permission and uses almost no power.

## 1. Principle

Public radio transmitters have their own backup power: a battery radio often picks them up when the phone no longer works. You only listen; you transmit nothing.

## 3. Step by step

### Step 1: Have a stand-alone radio

- **Action:** an FM radio powered by batteries, a crank (dynamo) or a solar panel. Check that it works with the mains unplugged.
- **Check:** it picks up a station without being plugged in.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 2: Write down the frequencies in advance

- **Action:** note on paper the FM frequencies of the public stations in your region: local public radio, national news. [TO CHECK: stations designated for crisis information in your area]
- **Check:** the paper list is stored with the radio.
- **Source:** [SOURCE NEEDED]

### Step 3: Improve reception

- **Action:** extend the aerial, move near a window or higher up, turn the radio slowly.
- **Check:** the sound is clear.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 4: Listen when the news is on

- **Action:** switch on at the top of the hour, when the news bulletins air, then switch off to save power.
- **Check:** you have heard a full bulletin.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 5: Write down what matters

- **Action:** note on paper the instructions you hear, the time and the station.
- **Check:** the instructions are written down and dated.
- **Source:** not applicable

### Step 6: Know the other alert channels

- **Action:** know that phones receive emergency alerts (FR-Alert in France, EU-Alert across Europe) as long as the mobile network works, and when your local sirens are tested (in France, the first Wednesday of the month at noon).
- **Check:** you can recognise a phone alert and the siren signal.
- **Source:** “Tous responsables” guide, SGDSN (see sources)

## 4. If you get stuck

- **Step 1 — the radio runs on batteries and there are none left.** Take them from a less useful object (remote control, toy) if the size matches.
- **Step 3 — nothing comes through indoors.** Try outside or higher up; thick walls block reception.
- **Step 6 — no alert on the phone.** The mobile network may be down: the radio is then the main source.
