# Insulate with mycelium

## 1. The idea

Mycelium, the underground part of fungi, forms a network of filaments that binds together the fibres it grows on. Grown on agricultural or urban waste (straw, hemp, sawdust, coffee grounds), then dried to stop its growth, it makes a light, insulating material. The most studied species is the oyster mushroom. The material is bio-based and compostable at the end of its life, and its raw material is local waste.

## 2. Where it stands

The material is at the prototype stage in France. In Normandy, the Station Hyphae laboratory, based in a community venue, began its work in 2026: experimentation, then qualification of insulation, panels and bricks, aiming to qualify materials for partition walls by 2030. In Charente, a mushroom grower makes straw and oyster-mushroom panels and is seeking to set up a cooperative. The announced performance (thermal conductivity comparable to wood fibre or hemp) still needs independent checking.

**Maturity: prototype.**

## 3. Who is working on it

- Station Hyphae, an applied research laboratory on mycelium in architecture and design, in Normandy.
- Mushroom growers and engineering schools, such as the “Mycélium Charente” project.
- Public laboratories working on bio-based materials. [SOURCE NEEDED: ongoing public research programmes]

## 4. Open questions

- Fire and moisture resistance in a real building.
- Durability over decades.
- No certification: a non-certified material cannot be used as insulation in a home.
- Reproducibility outside the lab: contamination by other moulds, complete drying.

## 5. How to contribute

- Grow small samples in a workshop, to learn and document, without installing them in a home.
- Connect a laboratory with farmers who have straw or hemp to put to use.
- Report your trials and their results to CivicNode, successful or not.

## 6. Caution

This lead describes ongoing research, not a method to apply. A non-certified material is not installed in an occupied building. Growing fungi releases spores: ventilate and avoid prolonged exposure, especially for people with allergies. [TO CHECK: workshop precautions]
